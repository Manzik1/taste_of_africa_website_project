About Tastes of Africa

Our mission is to celebrate, preserve, and share Africa's diverse food heritage through traditional recipes, regional dishes, and the stories behind the meals that bring communities together.

Our Mission

Africa has a rich variety of food traditions shaped by its different climates, communities, farming practices, and histories. From West African Jollof Rice to East African Isombe, many dishes carry stories about the people and places where they originated.

Tastes of Africa aims to make these food traditions easier to discover. We share recipes, explain traditional ingredients, and introduce visitors to the cultural background of African meals.

Whether you want to prepare a traditional dish, learn about a country's food culture, or explore new African flavours, this website is a place to start.

What We Do

  • Share traditional recipes such as Jollof Rice, Isombe, and Ugali.
  • Explain the cultural background and origins of African dishes.
  • Introduce local ingredients such as cassava, millet, plantains, and groundnuts.
  • Provide cooking videos and preparation instructions.
  • Explore food traditions from different African regions.
  • Encourage people to preserve and share African culinary heritage.

A Brief History of African Cuisine

Ancient Times

Early African Food Traditions

Early African communities prepared meals using locally available grains, roots, fruits, vegetables, fish, and meat. Foods such as millet, sorghum, yams, and leafy vegetables became important ingredients in many communities.

Around 3000 BCE

Ancient Egypt's Food Culture

Along the Nile, ancient Egyptians grew wheat and barley and prepared bread, porridge, and beer. Their diets also included lentils, onions, dates, fish, and fava beans. The Nile supported farming and food production.

From 700 CE

Trade and Cultural Exchange

Trade routes across the Sahara and along the East African coast connected African communities with Arabia and Asia. These exchanges influenced food traditions, including the use of spices, rice, and new cooking techniques.

1400s

West African Food Traditions

West African farming and trade supported the use of rice, millet, sorghum, groundnuts, fish, and vegetables. These ingredients helped shape regional meals, stews, and grain-based dishes.

1500s–1800s

New Ingredients in African Cooking

Exchange with the Americas introduced crops such as maize, cassava, tomatoes, and chilli peppers to African food systems. Over time, communities incorporated these ingredients into familiar dishes and developed new regional recipes.

Today

African Cuisine Goes Global

African food is increasingly enjoyed around the world. Dishes such as Jollof Rice, Isombe, Ugali, Pilau, and Amateke show the variety of ingredients and cooking traditions found across the continent. Restaurants, chefs, and home cooks continue to share these flavours with new audiences.
