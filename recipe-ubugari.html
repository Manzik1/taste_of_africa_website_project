Ubugari

A traditional Rwandan staple prepared from flour and hot water.

Ubugari is a filling staple eaten in Rwanda and made by cooking cassava flour with hot water until it forms a thick, smooth dough. The mixture is stirred continuously to prevent lumps and to achieve a firm texture. It is commonly shaped into portions before serving. Ubugari is usually eaten with Isombe, beans, vegetables or meat-based sauces. Its mild taste makes it a suitable accompaniment to dishes with rich flavours.

Cooking Instructions

View Cooking Instructions
  1. Bring water to a boil in a strong cooking pot.
  2. Reduce the heat slightly and gradually add the cassava flour while stirring continuously with a wooden spoon.
  3. Continue mixing to prevent lumps from forming.
  4. Keep stirring and pressing the mixture against the sides of the pot until it becomes thick and smooth.
  5. Allow it to cook over low heat for a few more minutes, stirring until the dough becomes firm.
  6. Remove from the heat and shape into portions.
  7. Serve warm with Isombe, beans, vegetables or your preferred sauce.

Country of Origin

Ubugari is enjoyed in Rwanda, in East Africa. Cassava is an important food crop in the region, and its flour is used to prepare filling meals served with vegetables, beans and sauces.
